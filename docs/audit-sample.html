A worked example, not a client engagement. The data is our own 44-row fixture (`examples/spend-sample.csv`); the output is the real CLI, unedited. We publish it so a buyer can see the shape of the deliverable before paying for one.

1. What was checked

Ten checks, each answering one question. A check that finds nothing says so.

  • Duplicate charge — same tx_hash paid twice
  • Price escalation — settled above quote
  • Failed but billed — error status, money taken
  • Missing refund — charged above actual, no refund line
  • Model overpay — paid above the median for the same tool and model
  • Budget exceeded — spend above the agent's limit
  • Runaway loop — calls per minute above threshold
  • Unknown asset / network — money moved outside the expected rails
  • Missing payee — no counterparty recorded
  • Unpriced call — spend with no reason attached

On a fixture this small the absolute numbers are not the point. The point is the ratio: four error classes fired on 44 rows. On a real month the same four checks decide whether an agent is a product or a leak.

5. What this report does not say

  • It does not verify the chain. v0.2 records onchain_verified=false; the rows are checked against the records you provide.
  • It does not claim the agent was wrong or unsafe. It records what was spent.
  • It does not say money was recovered. Nothing was recovered here.
  • It does not replace a provider invoice. Estimates are labelled; confirmed means matched against the counterparty.
  • If a check found nothing, the report says nothing was found for it — a missing finding is not a clean bill of health.

6. What to do next

  • Fix the duplicate first: it is a bug, not a price
  • Put a hard limit per agent, not per company
  • Record a reason for every call, or do not pay it
  • Alert on failed-but-billed: that is money for nothing
  • Re-run the audit in 30 days with the same columns
